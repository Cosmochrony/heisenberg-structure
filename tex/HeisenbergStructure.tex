  \begin{abstract}
    We prove that the admissibility axioms of the Cosmochrony framework,
    together with Born--Infeld parity, uniquely determine the algebraic structure
    of the admissible fibre $F_n$.
    The central result is that any structure of observable directions that is
    finite, minimal, and genuinely non-pre-resolved is necessarily of Heisenberg
    type: the symmetry group of $F_n$ is isomorphic to
    $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ for a prime $q$, and $F_n$ is
    isomorphic to the Weil representation $V_\rho$ on $L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
    The proof proceeds through a single structural lemma — admissible
    non-factorisability — which is a direct formalisation of Axiom A3, and
    requires no input beyond A1--A3, Born--Infeld parity, and classical
    classification results.
    As a corollary, the Heisenberg uncertainty principle is derived as a structural
    property of the non-resolved proto-state, not as an independent postulate
    about observables.
    This paper provides the detailed proof of the result stated in
    sketch form in the companion paper on admissible non-injective
    transitions~\cite{Foundation}.
  \end{abstract}

  \paragraph{Keywords.}
    Admissibility; non-factorisability; Heisenberg group; Weil representation;
    Stone--von Neumann theorem; Born--Infeld parity; proto-state; uncertainty principle;
    canonical quantisation; emergent quantum structure.

  \section{Introduction}
  \label{sec:intro}

  The Cosmochrony programme~\cite{Cosmochrony} derives quantum structure from a
  minimal relational foundation, without postulating a background spacetime,
  a Hilbert space, or an external substrate.
  Its primitive is the local structure of admissible transitions between
  observable states, governed by four axioms.

  The companion paper~\cite{Foundation} establishes that Axiom~A3 —
  admissibility as non-premature selection — forces phase coherence and identifies
  the admissible fibre $F_n$ as a precursor of Hilbert structure.
  It states, as a structural remark, that $F_n$ is concretely realised as the
  Weil representation $V_\rho$ of the discrete Heisenberg group
  $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.

  The present paper provides the detailed proof of this identification.
  The main result, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, is:

  \begin{quote}
    \textit{Under A1--A3 and Born--Infeld parity, the symmetry group of $F_n$ is
    isomorphic to $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ for some prime $q$,
      and $F_n \simeq V_\rho$.}
  \end{quote}

  \noindent
  The proof rests on a single structural lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:non-factorisation}),
  which formalises the prohibition on premature resolution encoded in A3.
  Once this lemma is established, the rest of the argument is a chain of
  classification steps: non-trivial commutator, central commutator, Heisenberg
  algebra, prime order of the centre, and Stone--von Neumann identification.

  \subsection{The four axioms}

    \begin{description}
      \item[\textbf{A1 — Local projective admissibility.}]
      For any observable state $O_{n-1}$, there exists a non-empty set $F_n$ of
      admissible successor directions.
      The set $F_n$ is defined by internal structural constraints (Born--Infeld
      bound, spectral envelope, symmetry requirements) and does not presuppose
      an ambient configuration space.

      \item[\textbf{A2 — Structural non-injectivity.}]
      Distinct admissible directions from $O_{n-1}$ may lead to the same resolved
      successor state $O_n$.

      \item[\textbf{A3 — Admissibility as non-premature selection.}]
      An admissible transition preserves the full multiplicity of open directions
      until projection locking occurs.
      It does not select among them prematurely — that is, before the
      Born--Infeld saturation threshold is reached.

      \item[\textbf{A4 — Projection locking.}]
      Resolution occurs only when continuous Born--Infeld saturation meets the
      discrete support of the observable image of the projection.
      In the spectral admissibility realisation, this support is the shell
      decomposition of the observable space.
    \end{description}

  \subsection{The admissible fibre and its generators}

    We assume throughout that the admissible fibre $F_n$ is finite-dimensional.
    This follows from the Born--Infeld admissibility bound~\cite{BornInfeld}:
    the constraint $A_n \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}} / \sqrt{\lambda_n}$ bounds the effective
    amplitude of each mode, and only finitely many modes satisfy it at any
    given cascade depth~$n$.

    By A1, the admissible fibre $F_n$ is a non-empty set of admissible directions
    from $O_{n-1}$.
    It carries a group of symmetry transformations, whose generators are constrained
    by the Born--Infeld bound and the spectral envelope.
    We denote the symmetry group of $F_n$ by $G_n$; its action on $F_n$ is by
    definition faithful.

    Born--Infeld parity~\cite{BornInfeld} asserts that the BI action satisfies
    $S_{\mathrm{BI}}[\chi] = S_{\mathrm{BI}}[-\chi]$, which forces the involution
    $\sigma\colon c \mapsto q - c$ on the central character of the Weil
    representation.
    At the level of generators, this means: for every generator $X$ of $G_n$,
    there is a conjugate generator $\sigma(X)$, the image of $X$ under $\sigma$.
    By A1, no generator beyond the conjugate pair $(X, \sigma(X))$ is required
    to generate the admissible symmetry group: the admissibility constraints —
    BI bound, spectral envelope, and symmetry requirements — are entirely
    encoded by the pair, and any additional independent generator would
    constitute structure not forced by these constraints.
    The pair $(X, \sigma(X))$ therefore forms the minimal admissible generating
    set of $G_n$.

    \begin{definition}[Stable decomposition]
      \label{def:stable}
      A direct sum decomposition $F_n = F^{(1)} \oplus F^{(2)}$ is called
      \emph{stable} (under admissible generators) if both $F^{(1)}$ and $F^{(2)}$
      are invariant under the action of every generator of $G_n$.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{definition}[Admissible resolution]
      \label{def:resolution}
      A direction $f \in F_n$ is \emph{admissibly resolved} if there exists a
      stable decomposition $F_n = F^{(1)} \oplus F^{(2)}$ such that
      $f \in F^{(1)}$ or $f \in F^{(2)}$, i.e.\ $f$ is assigned to a definite
      sector prior to any Born--Infeld saturation event.
    \end{definition}

  \subsection{The Weil representation}

    For a prime $q$ and a non-zero character $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$,
    the discrete Heisenberg group
    $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}) = \{(a,b,\gamma) : a,b,\gamma \in \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}\}$
    with multiplication
    $(a,b,\gamma)(a',b',\gamma') = (a+a',\, b+b',\, \gamma+\gamma'+ab')$
    admits an irreducible representation of dimension $q$, the Weil representation
    $\rho_c$, acting on $V_\rho = L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{C}^q$ by
    \begin{equation}
      \bigl(\rho_c(a,b,\gamma)\,f\bigr)(x)
      \;=\; e^{2\pi i c(\gamma + bx)/q}\,f(x - a).
      \label{eq:weil}
    \end{equation}
    The central element $(0,0,1)$ acts as multiplication by $e^{2\pi ic/q} \neq 1$
    (since $c \neq 0$), so the central character is non-trivial.

  \section{The Admissible Non-Factorisability Lemma}
  \label{sec:lemma}

  The following lemma is the structural core of the proof.
  It is a direct formalisation of Axiom A3.

  \begin{lemma}[Admissible non-factorisability]
    \label{lem:non-factorisation}
    Under A1--A3, every stable decomposition of $F_n$ is trivial, i.e.\ one of
    $F^{(1)}$ and $F^{(2)}$ must be $\{0\}$.
    In particular, the action of $G_n$ on $F_n$ is irreducible.
  \end{lemma}

  \begin{proof}
    Suppose $F_n = F^{(1)} \oplus F^{(2)}$ is a non-trivial stable decomposition,
    with $F^{(1)} \neq \{0\}$ and $F^{(2)} \neq \{0\}$.
    By Definition~\ref{def:stable}, both summands are invariant under every
    generator of $G_n$.
    Since the generators of $G_n$ are the constitutive elements of the
    admissibility structure of $F_n$ (A1), the decomposition is distinguished
    by the admissibility structure itself: the sector to which a direction $f$
    belongs ($F^{(1)}$ or $F^{(2)}$) is determined by an invariant internal to $F_n$.

    This constitutes an admissible resolution in the sense of
    Definition~\ref{def:resolution}: each open direction carries a definite
    sectorial label, accessible within the admissibility structure of $F_n$,
    prior to any Born--Infeld saturation event.

    A3 forbids premature selection among open directions: the transition must
    remain structurally neutral among all elements of $F_n$ until projection
    locking forces resolution.
    An admissible resolution prior to locking is precisely what A3 prohibits.
    Therefore the non-trivial stable decomposition is forbidden.

    Irreducibility follows immediately: any proper $G_n$-invariant subspace
    $W \subsetneq F_n$ with $W \neq \{0\}$ would yield a non-trivial stable
    decomposition $F_n = W \oplus W^\perp$ (since $G_n$ acts unitarily, the
    orthogonal complement of a $G_n$-invariant subspace is also $G_n$-invariant).
    This contradicts the non-factorisability just proved.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Lemma~\ref{lem:non-factorisation} as a restatement of A3]
    \label{rem:restatement}
    The content of Lemma~\ref{lem:non-factorisation} may be expressed as:
    \begin{equation}
      \text{proto-state genuinely non-resolved}
      \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
      F_n \text{ carries no admissible sectorial decomposition.}
    \end{equation}
    In this sense, the lemma is not an additional hypothesis: it is A3 rephrased
    in the language of representation theory.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Unitarity of the $G_n$ action]
    \label{rem:unitarity}
    The action of $G_n$ on $F_n$ is unitary.
    This follows from the BI bound: admissible transformations preserve the
    norm structure of $F_n$, since the BI saturation threshold is defined in
    terms of mode amplitudes $\|f\|$~\cite{BornInfeld}.
    Unitarity is used in the proof above to ensure that the orthogonal complement
    of an invariant subspace is again invariant.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Proof of the Main Theorem}
  \label{sec:main}

  \begin{theorem}[A1--A3 and BI parity force Heisenberg structure]
    \label{thm:main}
    Under A1--A3 and Born--Infeld parity, the symmetry group $G_n$ of the
    admissible fibre $F_n$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$
    for some prime $q$.
    Consequently, $F_n$ is isomorphic to the Weil representation $V_\rho$ on
    $L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
  \end{theorem}

  The proof proceeds in five steps.

  \subsection{Step 1: Non-trivial commutator}

    \begin{proposition}
      \label{prop:commutator}
      Under A1--A3, the conjugate generators $X$ and $\sigma(X)$ satisfy
      $[X,\,\sigma(X)] \neq 0$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Suppose $[X,\,\sigma(X)] = 0$.
      Then $X$ and $\sigma(X)$ are commuting unitary operators on the
      finite-dimensional space $F_n$.
      By the spectral theorem for commuting normal operators, they admit a
      simultaneous eigenspace decomposition:
      \begin{equation}
        F_n \;=\; \bigoplus_\lambda F_\lambda,
      \end{equation}
      where $F_\lambda$ is the common eigenspace for the pair of eigenvalues
      $(x_\lambda,\, y_\lambda) \in \mathrm{U}(1)^2$ of $(X,\, \sigma(X))$.
      This decomposition is non-trivial (since $X$ and $\sigma(X)$ are generators
      of $G_n$, they are not scalar multiples of the identity by faithfulness of
      the action).
      Each summand $F_\lambda$ is invariant under $X$ and $\sigma(X)$, and hence,
      since $(X,\, \sigma(X))$ is the minimal generating set of $G_n$ by A1, under
      all of $G_n$.
      Therefore the decomposition is stable.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:non-factorisation}, a non-trivial stable decomposition is
      forbidden.
      Contradiction.
      Therefore $[X,\, \sigma(X)] \neq 0$.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Step 2: Central commutator}

    \begin{proposition}
      \label{prop:central}
      Let $Z := [X,\,\sigma(X)]$.
      Under A1, $Z$ is central in $G_n$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Suppose $Z$ is not central.
      Then there exists a generator $Y \in G_n$ such that $[Z,\, Y] \neq 0$.
      The group generated by $(X,\, \sigma(X),\, Z,\, Y)$ is then strictly richer
      than the group generated by the conjugate pair $(X,\, \sigma(X))$ alone: the
      pair $(Z,\, Y)$ with $[Z,\, Y] \neq 0$ constitutes an independent non-abelian
      sub-structure not generated by the commutator $Z = [X,\, \sigma(X)]$.
      Concretely, the iterated commutator $[[X,\, \sigma(X)],\, Y]$ is non-trivial
      and independent of the generators already present, so the derived series of
      $G_n$ does not terminate at the subgroup $\langle Z \rangle$: the group is
      not nilpotent of class~2 over the conjugate pair.
      This produces a strictly larger derived subalgebra than that generated
      by the pair $(X,\, \sigma(X))$ alone, contradicting the assumption that
      all admissible relations are generated by this pair under A1.

      By A1, $F_n$ is generated minimally by the conjugate pair $(X,\, \sigma(X))$:
      the admissibility constraints force no generator beyond those required by
      Born--Infeld parity, and the algebra of $G_n$ is entirely determined by the
      BI-admissible structure of the pair.
      An independent non-abelian sub-structure beyond what the pair generates would
      constitute an internal structure of $F_n$ not determined by the admissibility
      constraints, contradicting the minimality of A1.
      Therefore $Z$ is central in $G_n$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Nilpotency class 2]
      \label{rem:nilpotency}
      Propositions~\ref{prop:commutator} and~\ref{prop:central} together imply
      that $G_n$ is nilpotent of class exactly 2: the commutator subgroup is
      $[G_n, G_n] = \langle Z \rangle$, which is central and non-trivial.
      This is precisely the nilpotency class of the Heisenberg group.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Step 3: Classification as the Heisenberg algebra}

    \begin{proposition}
      \label{prop:heisenberg-algebra}
      The group $G_n$ is isomorphic to a Heisenberg group.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      By Propositions~\ref{prop:commutator} and~\ref{prop:central}, $G_n$ is a
      group with:
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
      \item a generating pair $(X,\, \sigma(X))$ related by the involution $\sigma$;
      \item a non-trivial central commutator $Z = [X,\, \sigma(X)]$;
      \item no independent algebraic relation beyond those forced by minimality (A1).
      \end{enumerate}
      Condition~(iii) is made precise as follows: minimality (A1) excludes any
      additional independent commutation relation beyond those generated by the
      conjugate pair $(X,\, \sigma(X))$, so the only non-trivial relation in $G_n$
      is $[X,\, \sigma(X)] = Z$ with $Z$ central.
      Conditions (i)--(iii) mean that $G_n$ is a non-split central extension of the
      abelian group $\langle X \rangle \times \langle \sigma(X)\rangle$ by the
      cyclic group $\langle Z \rangle$, with the extension class determined by the
      commutator pairing $(X,\, \sigma(X)) \mapsto Z$.
      Since no additional relation is present, this is the \emph{universal} central
      extension of $\langle X \rangle \times \langle \sigma(X)\rangle$ with the
      given commutator.

      The group cohomology classification of central extensions of
      $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$ by $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$
      is given by $H^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z},\,
      \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$~\cite{Brown1982},
      generated by the class corresponding to the standard symplectic commutator
      $[(a,0),\,(0,b)] = ab \bmod q$.
      Up to automorphism of $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^2$, all non-degenerate
      alternating bilinear forms are equivalent (since $q$ is prime, hence
      $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^2$ is a two-dimensional symplectic space over
      $\mathbb{F}_q$, and $\mathrm{Sp}(2, \mathbb{F}_q)$ acts transitively on
      non-degenerate forms), yielding a unique non-split extension class.
      The unique non-split extension (up to isomorphism) is the Heisenberg group:
      \begin{equation}
        G_n \;\cong\;
        \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
        \;=\;
        \bigl\langle X,\, \sigma(X),\, Z \;\big|\;
        [X,\, \sigma(X)] = Z,\; Z \text{ central},\;
        X^q = \sigma(X)^q = Z^q = 1 \bigr\rangle.
        \label{eq:heisenberg-presentation}
      \end{equation}
      The commutator defines a non-degenerate alternating bilinear form
      $\omega \colon \langle X \rangle \times \langle \sigma(X) \rangle \to \langle Z \rangle$
      by $\omega(X^a,\, \sigma(X)^b) = Z^{ab}$ on the generator space.
      Non-degeneracy follows from $Z \neq 0$ and the fact that $X$ and $\sigma(X)$
      are independent generators: $\omega(X, \cdot) \neq 0$ and
      $\omega(\cdot,\, \sigma(X)) \neq 0$.
      This form fixes the Heisenberg structure up to isomorphism.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Step 4: The order of $Z$ is prime}

    \begin{proposition}
      \label{prop:prime}
      The order $|Z|$ of the central generator $Z$ is prime.
      Hence $q$ in~\eqref{eq:heisenberg-presentation} is prime.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Write $|Z| = \prod_i p_i^{a_i}$ for the prime factorisation.
      Suppose $|Z|$ is not prime.
      Then the centre $\langle Z \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/|Z|\mathbb{Z}$ contains
      at least one proper non-trivial subgroup, say $H = \langle Z^d \rangle$ for
      some proper divisor $d > 1$ of $|Z|$.

      Since $H$ is a subgroup of the centre $Z(G_n)$, it is normal in $G_n$.
      The quotient map $\pi \colon G_n \to G_n/H$ induces a decomposition of any
      $G_n$-representation via the characters of $H$.
      More precisely, since $H$ acts as scalars (being central), Schur's lemma
      implies that any irreducible $G_n$-representation on which $H$ acts trivially
      factors through $G_n/H$, while any representation on which $H$ acts
      non-trivially decomposes $F_n$ into $H$-eigenspaces:
      \begin{equation}
        F_n \;=\; \bigoplus_{\chi \in \hat{H}} F_\chi,
      \end{equation}
      where $F_\chi = \{v \in F_n : h \cdot v = \chi(h)\,v \;\forall\, h \in H\}$
      and $\hat{H}$ denotes the character group of $H$.
      If $|H| > 1$, this decomposition is non-trivial (there is more than one
      $H$-eigenspace, since $F_n$ is not a direct sum of trivial $H$-representations
      — $Z$ acts non-trivially on $F_n$ as the commutator of generators).

      Each $H$-eigenspace $F_\chi$ is invariant under the full action of $G_n$:
      for $g \in G_n$ and $v \in F_\chi$, and $h \in H \leq Z(G_n)$,
      \begin{equation}
        h \cdot (g \cdot v) \;=\; g \cdot (h \cdot v) \;=\; \chi(h)\,(g \cdot v),
      \end{equation}
      so $g \cdot v \in F_\chi$.
      Therefore the decomposition $F_n = \bigoplus_\chi F_\chi$ is stable under all
      of $G_n$, hence under admissible generators.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:non-factorisation}, this stable decomposition is forbidden.
      Therefore $|H| = 1$, i.e.\ $\langle Z\rangle$ has no proper non-trivial
      subgroup.
      A cyclic group with no proper non-trivial subgroup is cyclic of prime order.
      Hence $|Z| = q$ is prime.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Step 5: Identification $F_n \simeq V_\rho$}

    \begin{proposition}
      \label{prop:vrho}
      $F_n$ is isomorphic to the Weil representation $V_\rho$ of
      $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      By Propositions~\ref{prop:heisenberg-algebra} and~\ref{prop:prime},
      $G_n \cong \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ for a prime $q$.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:non-factorisation}, $F_n$ is an irreducible
      $G_n$-representation.
      It remains to identify which irreducible representation of
      $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ it is.

      By the discrete Stone--von Neumann theorem (see e.g.~\cite{Gerardin1977}),
      $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ has, for each non-zero character
      $c \in (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$, a unique irreducible representation
      of dimension $q$ with central character $\chi_c \colon (0,0,\gamma) \mapsto
      e^{2\pi ic\gamma/q}$.
      All irreducible representations with non-trivial central character are of this
      form (up to unitary equivalence), and they are all isomorphic to the Weil
      representation~\eqref{eq:weil}.
      In addition, $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ has $q^2$
      one-dimensional representations (those factoring through the abelianisation),
      but these are excluded by irreducibility of $F_n$ together with
      $q \geq 2$ (since $\dim F_n \geq 2$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:commutator}:
      a one-dimensional representation would be abelian, contradicting $[X,\sigma(X)] \neq 0$).

      Born--Infeld parity forces $c \neq 0$~\cite{BornInfeld}: the parity involution
      $\sigma\colon c \mapsto q-c$ has no fixed point in
      $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$ (since $q$ is odd prime and $q-c \not\equiv c$
      for $c \not\equiv 0$), so any admissible character is non-trivial.
      Therefore $F_n$ carries a non-trivial central character, and by the
      Stone--von Neumann theorem, $F_n \simeq V_\rho$.
    \end{proof}

    \noindent
    Propositions~\ref{prop:commutator}--\ref{prop:vrho} together constitute the
    proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. \hfill$\square$

    \begin{remark}[Summary of the derivation chain]
      \label{rem:chain}
      The logical chain of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is:
      \begin{equation}
        \begin{aligned}
          \mathrm{A3}
          &\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{no stable decomposition (Lem.~\ref{lem:non-factorisation})}
          \;\Longrightarrow\; [X,\sigma(X)] \neq 0 \text{ (Prop.~\ref{prop:commutator})},\\
          \mathrm{A1}
          &\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{minimal generative closure}
          \;\Longrightarrow\; Z \text{ central (Prop.~\ref{prop:central})},\\
          \mathrm{A1}
          &\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{no excess relation}
          \;\Longrightarrow\; G_n \cong \mathrm{Heis}_3 \text{ (Prop.~\ref{prop:heisenberg-algebra})},\\
          \mathrm{A3}
          &\;\Longrightarrow\; \text{irreducibility}
          \;\Longrightarrow\; |Z| = q \text{ prime (Prop.~\ref{prop:prime})},\\
          \text{BI parity}
          &\;\Longrightarrow\; c \neq 0
          \;\Longrightarrow\; F_n \simeq V_\rho \text{ (Prop.~\ref{prop:vrho}).}
        \end{aligned}
      \end{equation}
      Every arrow is either a direct consequence of an axiom or a classical
      algebraic fact.
      No external input is required beyond A1--A3 and Born--Infeld parity.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{The uncertainty principle as a structural corollary}

    \begin{corollary}[Heisenberg uncertainty principle]
      \label{cor:uncertainty}
      Under A1--A3 and Born--Infeld parity, conjugate admissible observables
      $X$ and $\sigma(X)$ satisfy
      \begin{equation}
        \Delta X \cdot \Delta \sigma(X) \;\geq\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,|{\langle [X,\,\sigma(X)]\rangle}|
        \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}|\langle Z\rangle|
        \;>\; 0.
      \end{equation}
      In particular, $X$ and $\sigma(X)$ cannot be simultaneously sharp.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      By Proposition~\ref{prop:commutator}, $[X,\, \sigma(X)] = Z \neq 0$.
      The standard Robertson uncertainty relation for unitary operators on a
      Hilbert space gives the bound above.
      The strict positivity follows from $Z \neq 0$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Uncertainty as a property of the non-resolved proto-state]
      \label{rem:uncertainty-interpretation}
      Corollary~\ref{cor:uncertainty} establishes that the Heisenberg uncertainty
      principle is not a property of the observables per se, but a structural
      consequence of A3 alone: the prohibition on premature resolution forces
      $[X,\, \sigma(X)] \neq 0$, from which the uncertainty bound follows.
      Equivalently, the uncertainty principle is the algebraic signature of the
      fact that the proto-state is genuinely non-resolved: if the proto-state were
      resolved (in the sense of carrying a stable sectorial decomposition), the
      commutator would be zero and simultaneous sharp values would be possible.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Canonical quantisation as structural necessity}

    \begin{corollary}[Canonical quantisation]
      \label{cor:quantisation}
      Under A1--A3 and Born--Infeld parity, the algebra of admissible observables
      on $F_n$ is the canonical quantisation of the discrete symplectic space
      $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^2$ with symplectic form $\omega(X,\,\sigma(X)) = Z$.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      By Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, $G_n \cong \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$
      and $F_n \simeq V_\rho$.
      The Heisenberg group is, by construction, the canonical quantisation of the
      symplectic space $((\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^2,\, \omega)$, in the sense that
      it is the unique group whose Lie algebra (or discrete analogue) encodes the
      commutation relation $[X,\, \sigma(X)] = Z$ with $Z$ central and $\omega$
      non-degenerate.
      The Weil representation $V_\rho$ is the unique faithful irreducible
      representation of this quantisation.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Relation to the O-series}

    \begin{remark}[Non-abelian support from O23]
      \label{rem:nonabelian}
      Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is complementary to the result of~\cite{O23}, which
      establishes via the Hurwitz classification that the admissible neutral sector
      of $F_n$ is non-abelian (quaternionic).
      The present paper establishes the group structure of $G_n$ directly from
      A1--A3 without assuming the Hurwitz classification, using only the
      commutation argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:commutator}.
      The two derivations are therefore independent and mutually reinforcing:
      O23 establishes why the neutral sector is quaternionic and three-dimensional;
      the present paper establishes why the ambient symmetry group is Heisenberg
      and the representation is the Weil representation.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Discussion}
  \label{sec:discussion}

  \subsection{What the theorem establishes}

    Theorem~\ref{thm:main} establishes three things simultaneously.

    First, it closes the identification $F_n \simeq V_\rho$, which was stated
    as a structural remark in~\cite{Foundation} and as the target of open
    question Q5 therein.
    The space in which the proto-state lives between transitions is the Weil
    representation $V_\rho$ of $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$,
    uniquely determined by the axioms.
    This space is real in the sense of the Cosmochrony framework — it is not
    spatial, but it is not epistemic either: it is the space of structurally
    admissible directions, induced by the admissibility constraints themselves.

    Second, it derives canonical quantisation as a structural necessity rather
    than a modelling choice.
    The Heisenberg commutation relation $[X,\, \sigma(X)] = Z$ does not enter
    as a postulate; it is forced by A3 via the non-factorisability lemma.
    The imaginary unit and the complex Hilbert space structure follow from this
    relation, not from an independent axiom.

    Third, it resolves the open question Q6 of~\cite{Foundation}: the
    Heisenberg uncertainty principle is derived as a corollary of A3 alone,
    without any assumption about the nature of the observables.

  \subsection{The single non-trivial input}

    The proof rests on exactly one non-trivial structural claim: the admissible
    non-factorisability lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:non-factorisation}).
    All other steps use standard algebraic tools (simultaneous diagonalisation,
    group cohomology classification, Stone--von Neumann).

    Lemma~\ref{lem:non-factorisation} itself is a direct formalisation of A3.
    Its proof requires: (a) unitarity of the $G_n$ action (from the BI bound),
    (b) the definition of admissible generators as constitutive of the
    admissibility structure (A1), and (c) the prohibition on premature selection
    (A3).
    None of these is new: (a) is established in~\cite{BornInfeld}, (b) is the
    content of A1, and (c) is A3.

  \subsection{Open direction: the large-$q$ limit}

    Theorem~\ref{thm:main} establishes the structure of $F_n$ at finite prime $q$.
    The large-$q$ limit — in which $\mathbb{C}^q \to L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and the
    Weil representation approaches the metaplectic representation of the real
    Heisenberg group $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ — is the subject of the
    companion spectral geometry programme~\cite{Cosmochrony}.
    The emergence of continuous spacetime geometry from the BFS shell structure
    of $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ in the large-$q$ limit (with
    homogeneous dimension $D = 4$ from the Bass--Guivarc'h theorem) remains an
    open theorem.
